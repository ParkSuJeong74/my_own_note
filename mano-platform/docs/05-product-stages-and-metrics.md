# Product stages and metrics

## Guiding sequence

Mano grows in one direction: **private note → intentional publication → community connection**.
Later stages must not weaken private/offline use, and their code should remain dormant or removable
when a deployment only needs the note workspace.

Dates in the legacy plan are estimates, not commitments. Progress is controlled by readiness gates.

## Stage 1 — personal note and writing workspace

Primary users are writers, researchers/students, office workers and people keeping journals,
reading notes or hobby records.

Minimum useful outcome:

- dependable block/Markdown editing, folders, tags and search;
- automatic local durability, offline operation, synchronization and recovery;
- import/export and version history;
- writer tools: word count, writing goals, progress, chapters and story-reference notes;
- accessible keyboard, touch and responsive web workflows.

Readiness gate for Stage 2:

- the owner uses Mano regularly without returning to the legacy pages for core note work;
- restore, offline reconnect and conflict scenarios pass automated tests;
- publishing can be added through a stable document contract rather than editor-specific storage.

Historical business hypotheses: free basics and a low-cost premium plan for capacity, templates and
support. Pricing is not approved until storage/support costs and legal obligations are known.

## Stage 2 — publishing and blog

A private page can be published explicitly, previewed, scheduled, updated or withdrawn. Publication
must never accidentally expose private descendants, attachments, comments or revisions.

Capabilities include themes, profile, categories/series, verified domains, comments, likes,
bookmarks, subscriptions/newsletters, SEO and privacy-aware analytics.

Readiness gate for Stage 3:

- publishing and moderation are reliable enough for external readers;
- identity, notification, abuse-reporting and permission foundations are proven;
- usage demonstrates recurring interaction, not only page views.

Legacy indicators to validate rather than promise: 5,000 monthly published posts, 100 average views
per post and 10 subscribers per blogger.

## Stage 3 — social and communities

This stage combines a follow-based public feed with opt-in topic communities. Spaces contain
channels, roles, threaded discussions and events. Private notes, published posts, short posts and
community messages remain distinct content types with explicit conversion actions.

Potential later capabilities include voice/video, screen sharing, workshops, mentoring and unified
creator portfolios. These require separate safety, moderation, recording-consent and cost reviews.

The legacy proposal uses 100,000 MAU and 1,000 daily posts as an entry signal, then lists one million
MAU, one million daily interactions and KRW 100 million monthly revenue as success targets. They are
planning hypotheses and require validated definitions, measurement windows and privacy-safe
instrumentation.

## Scenario continuity

- A writer captures an idea privately, discusses a deliberately shared excerpt, publishes a refined
  post and receives feedback without exposing the source notebook.
- A reading-group operator schedules a discussion, archives selected outcomes, and publishes an
  edited summary only after participants' sharing rules are satisfied.

These scenarios define the desired continuity; they do not imply that notes, chat messages and posts
share one permissive database record.
